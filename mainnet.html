All Ethereum mainnet smart contracts that are currently in scope for the Origin Bug Bounty program.

🔗 Mainnet Overview

Origin Protocol's mainnet deployment runs on Ethereum Mainnet. The suite includes the Origin Dollar (OUSD) stablecoin, Origin Ether (OETH), and supporting infrastructure such as the Vault, various yield strategies, governance contracts, and the OGN token.

Researchers are expected to work with local forks of mainnet when testing. Any testing directly against live mainnet contracts is strictly prohibited and will void eligibility for rewards. All critical and high-severity findings must include a runnable Proof of Concept (PoC).

The contracts listed on this page represent the primary attack surface for smart contract vulnerabilities. The team uses a multi-sig governance model, and any vulnerability that can bypass admin controls or drain funds is considered Critical severity.

💲 OUSD Contracts

Origin Dollar (OUSD) is a yield-bearing stablecoin that automatically accrues yield from multiple DeFi protocols. The OUSD system consists of the token contract, a Vault that manages backing stablecoins, and a set of Strategy contracts that deploy capital.

⟠ OETH Contracts

Origin Ether (OETH) is a liquid staking token that generates yield from ETH staking and liquidity provision. OETH is backed by stETH, rETH, frxETH, and native ETH. The architecture mirrors OUSD but is optimised for ETH-denominated assets and LST integrations.

Security researchers should pay particular attention to the rebase mechanism, the price oracle integrations used by the Vault, and any cross-strategy interactions that could result in incorrect share accounting or loss of funds.

🗳 Governance & OGN

Origin's governance contracts control critical system parameters through a timelock and multi-sig structure. The OGN token powers governance participation. Vulnerabilities in governance that allow unauthorized actors to execute proposals or bypass timelocks are classified as Critical severity.

🎯 In-Scope Impacts (Mainnet)

The following impact categories are considered in-scope for mainnet contract vulnerabilities:

  • Direct theft or permanent freezing of user funds
  • Theft of unclaimed yield exceeding 10% of total yield
  • Protocol insolvency or manipulation of OUSD/OETH peg
  • Unauthorized execution of governance actions or admin functions
  • Smart contract bugs enabling re-entrancy or flash-loan exploits
  • Oracle manipulation leading to incorrect price calculations in the Vault
  • Bypassing timelock or multi-sig restrictions
